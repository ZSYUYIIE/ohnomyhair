# Global (non-university, non-country) funding sources and founder-stipend benchmarks in USD for a two-person indie team polishing a finished Godot 4 co-op puzzle platformer for a 12-week itch.io + Steam release (checked 19 Oct 2026)

Scope note: all figures are in USD unless marked. Where a source gives another currency, I convert at an assumed rate and say so (the rates are mine, not sourced). Earlier Singapore and university material is deliberately left out.

## Q1. What do tiny indie teams (1-3 people) pay themselves or budget as founder stipends per month, in USD?

### Takeaway
The most-cited international benchmark for small or first-time indie teams is Rami Ismail's default of **US$1,600-2,200 per person per month**. He calls US$2,200 a "globally average" indie salary. A stipend of **about US$2,000/month per founder** sits inside that range, so an international reviewer should find it credible. Publisher-side "fully loaded" rates of about US$10,000 per person-month, and US salaried pay (median US$129k/yr), are ceilings for comparison only, not stipend targets.

### Cited Findings
**Founder or indie salary defaults used in budgeting guides**
- Rami Ismail (Budget Viability, 2021): "For smaller projects with younger developers, I tend to use anywhere between **$1,600 & $2,200 of monthly salary** as a default if the team hasn't discussed this yet." He adds that, depending on where the team lives, this default "can either be ridiculously high or not nearly enough money." — [Levelling The Playing Field: Budget Viability](https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/budget-viability)
- Same guide, worked example: Among Us took 3 developers about 18 months. Budgeted at a "**globally average $2,200 salary** for independent developers that all own part of the company", that is $6,600/month and about $120,000 for the project. With a **30% safety buffer** the budget becomes about $160,000. — [LTPF: Budget Viability](https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/budget-viability); repeated in [LTPF: The Publisher's Lie (1 May 2023)](https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/the-publishers-lie/)
  - Arithmetic caveat (mine): $6,600 × 18 months = $118,800, and 30% on $120k is $156k, so the "$160k" is rounded.
- Ismail's budgeting method: list each person, their monthly salary and months worked, and an FTE fraction (1.0 FTE = 40 h/week; 0.5 = 20 h/week). Then add a 30% margin. "That's what you should be asking for at the least." — [LTPF: Budget Viability](https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/budget-viability)
- Ismail says budgets of "**$75,000 and $250,000** are incredibly common for small indie teams on their first few projects". — [LTPF: Budget Viability](https://ltpf.ramiismail.com/budget-viability)
- Bithell Games COO Alexander Sliwinski: publishers typically advise adding **10-20%** contingency for new teams. He points to the free budget templates at gamedevbizbook.com (The GameDev Business Handbook). — [GamesIndustry.biz, 8 Nov 2022](https://www.gamesindustry.biz/the-hidden-costs-of-indie-game-development)
- SteamPageAnalyzer (2026) uses "a **modest $40,000 salary**" per founder (about US$3,333/month) for a 3-person studio example. It notes such a studio needs about $400k gross per two-year cycle, roughly a 92nd-percentile Steam outcome. — [SteamPageAnalyzer, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-much-do-indie-game-developers-make)
- A financial-model vendor suggests $2,000-4,000/month per founder as a "modest salary". This is low-quality, generic startup content, seen only in a search summary. — [financialmodel.net](https://financialmodel.net/blogs/cost-open/indie-game-development) (treat as weak)

**Publisher and fully loaded rates (ceilings, not stipends)**
- Greg Lobanov (Wandersong, Chicory), as summarised by SteamPageAnalyzer: publishers budget roughly **$10,000 per person per full-time month** of remaining development, about $50,000 per platform for console ports, and about $2,500 per language for localising a 25,000-word game. — [SteamPageAnalyzer, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-publisher-vs-self-publishing)
- A developer's publisher guide on Medium (undated, single author) calls $10,000 USD per person per full-time month standard "or even considered kind of a low-ball by some". — [Medium: An Indie Developer's Guide to Publishers](https://medium.com/@banovg/an-indie-developers-guide-to-publishers-eb896a05353)
- UMBC game-production course notes give a rule of thumb of **$10-15k per developer per month**, including all per-developer costs (salary, taxes, benefits, hardware, overhead). They add that indies "often skimp". Ballpark: 3 people for 3 months is about $100k. — [UMBC CMSC 493 notes](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/olano/class/493-24-2/budgets.md.html)

**Surveys (context for "below market")**
- GDC 2025 Game Industry Salary Report (562 US respondents, July 2025): average salary about **$142,000**, median about **$129,000**. 50% of indie-studio respondents earn $100k+, against 85% at AAA studios. 5% earned under $15,000. — [GDC article](https://gdconf.com/article/us-game-development-salaries-in-2025-what-our-latest-industry-report-reveals/); [GamesIndustry.biz, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.gamesindustry.biz/gdc-survey-average-salary-for-us-games-professionals-was-142000-in-2025); [report PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/4035aceb-a7d8-4c07-ac70-57e54f473744_541450_GDC25_PDF_Game_Dev_Salary_Report.pdf)
  - These are US salaried staff, not founders. The survey is US-only.
- GDC 2025 State of the Game Industry (3,000+ developers, 86 countries): **82% of indie developers have put their own money into their games**. — [GDC SOTI 2025 PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/0794a269-d5c4-4994-9bcf-8c5730d0815e_2025_GDC_State_of_the_Game_Industry_report-1.pdf)
- GDC 2026 State of the Game Industry (2,300+ respondents, as summarised by SteamPageAnalyzer): 35% rely primarily on self-funding, 20% on publisher or project funding. — [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-much-do-indie-game-developers-make)
- Same report: 77% of younger indie studios (under 5 years old) have fewer than 20 employees, and 11% are solo. **Godot is used by 11% of developers at newer indie studios.** — [GDC SOTI 2026 PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-29-dec052f4_d88e_48ce_9f83_a18ce2f2a6e5_541400_GDC26_PDF_SOTI_Report.pdf)
- SteamPageAnalyzer's 2026 income estimate (its own model, no published sample): median Steam game lifetime gross about $1,136, 80th percentile about $34k, 90th about $198k. A full-time solo developer at the 100+ review tier earns about $33k/yr effective. — [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-much-do-indie-game-developers-make)

### Inferences
- **Recommended stipend: US$2,000 per founder per month (full-time, 1.0 FTE).** That is the middle-to-top of Ismail's US$1,600-2,200 default, below his US$2,200 "global average", and about one-fifth of the US$10k/person-month publisher rate.
  - Low case: US$1,600 (Ismail's floor).
  - High case: US$2,200 (Ismail's global average).
  - A reviewer can check it against a named, widely cited indie source.
- 12 weeks is about 2.76 months (84 days ÷ 30.44). For 2 founders (my arithmetic):
  - US$1,600/mo: about **$8,830**
  - US$2,000/mo: about **$11,040**
  - US$2,200/mo: about **$12,140**
  - At 0.5 FTE (20 h/week), halve these: about $4,400-6,100.
- If the founders budget at 0.5 FTE, US$2,000/mo full-time works out to about $11.50/hour at 20 h/week (about 87 h/month). That is easy to defend.
- Add Ismail's 30% margin, or the 10-20% contingency publishers advise, on top of the stipend plus external costs. Present founder time valued at market rate (for example, $10k/person-month) separately as in-kind contribution, not as cash requested.

### Gaps
- I did not get the actual GDC 2026 State of the Industry text on founder pay or indie income; the 35%/20% split comes from a secondary summary. GDC surveys do not report founder stipends as such.
- I found no funder or publisher that publishes an official "accepted cost-of-living rate" for founders. WINGS and Outersloth require a budget breakdown but publish no rate cards (see Q2).
- I did not open the gamedevbizbook.com budget templates or howtomarketagame.com, so they are not cited.

## Q2. Which engine-agnostic, international funding sources can a Godot game team like this realistically get, and what do they actually offer?

### Takeaway
For a US$5k-15k ask, the realistic paths are self-funding plus **Kickstarter** (if a founder lives in one of Kickstarter's 25 supported countries), small **festival prizes** (IGF, BIC, A MAZE), and **itch.io/Patreon** income. **WINGS finishing funds** fit the timing well, but only for teams led or co-founded by women or gender-marginalised developers. **Epic MegaGrants does not fund Godot games** unless the team ports to Unreal. Publishers and funds (Outersloth, Indie Fund, Kowloon Nights, the Voyer data) typically operate at US$20k-2M, with first-time teams finding money scarce in 2026.

### Cited Findings

#### Epic MegaGrants: needs Unreal, so not a fit for Godot
- The programme funds "projects built in and around Unreal Engine or projects that enhance the open source 3D graphics ecosystem". "Our average grants range from **$5,000 to $75,000**, with some extraordinary projects receiving up to $150,000." Recipients keep their IP. — [Epic MegaGrants](https://www.unrealengine.com/megagrants)
- Indie games track: "If you're a small or solo dev team working on a promising game **in Unreal Engine**..." — [Epic MegaGrants](https://www.unrealengine.com/megagrants)
- Engine switchers: "If you have a promising concept partially developed in another engine and are interested in **switching to Unreal Engine**, we reserve a proportion of MegaGrants that might be able to help with your transition." — [Epic MegaGrants](https://www.unrealengine.com/megagrants)
- The page lists an FAQ "Is my non-Unreal-Engine project eligible?", but its answer text did not load in any fetch. A third-party search of FAQs also found no source saying engine-only games outside Unreal qualify. — [Epic MegaGrants](https://www.unrealengine.com/megagrants)
- 2026 cycles:
  - Cycle 1: submissions 12 Jan-20 Mar, notices 15-19 Jun.
  - Cycle 2: submissions 29 Jun-4 Sep, notices 7-11 Dec.
  - Both 2026 windows are closed, and no 2027 dates are published yet. — [Epic MegaGrants](https://www.unrealengine.com/megagrants)
- Cycle-based submissions began on 12 Feb 2025. Before that, MegaGrants ran on a rolling basis. — [Unreal Engine news, 7 Feb 2025](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/changes-coming-to-epic-megagrants-submissions-in-2025)

#### Godot-specific money: none for games
- The Godot Foundation is a Dutch non-profit (Stichting Godot) that manages donations "used to enhance the Godot Engine". It does not fund game projects. — [Godot Foundation](https://godot.foundation/)

#### Kickstarter
- **Fees**: 5% platform fee plus payment processing, charged only if the campaign is funded.
  - US processing: 3% + $0.30 per pledge.
  - Pledges under $10: 5% + $0.08.
  - No fees if the goal is missed.
  - Sources: [Kickstarter Fees (US)](https://www.kickstarter.com/help/fees?country=US); [Help Center, 2 Sep 2026](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236674-what-are-the-fees)
- **Eligibility** (Help Center, 6 Aug 2026): creators must be verified in one of 25 countries:
  - Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, France, Germany, Greece, Hong Kong, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Poland, Singapore, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, UK, US.
  - An individual must be 18+, a resident of the launch country with that country's government ID, and hold a bank account in their own name there.
  - **No company is required.** Kickstarter suggests a Stripe Atlas US company as a workaround for other countries.
  - Source: [Kickstarter Help: Who can use Kickstarter?](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005128594-Who-can-use-Kickstarter)
- **Video-game success rate**: about **32%** overall in Narwhal's analysis of about 17,000 Video Games campaigns since 2009.
  - US$377M raised across 5,526 funded campaigns (average $68.2K).
  - 2024 total was $25.2M, down from $51M in 2013.
  - February is the best launch month (35% success), December the worst (28%).
  - Source: [GameDev Reports, 6 Nov 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-in-2025-and-before)
- 2025: 443 successful video-game campaigns raised "just shy of 2024's roughly $26 million". Cinevva also says "most successful campaigns raise between $10,000 and $50,000". This is a secondary guide with no stated method. — [Cinevva, 26 Jan 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games)
- Kickstarter says Q1 2026 was the strongest first quarter for video games in 13 years: four campaigns over $500k, two over $1M. — [Kickstarter blog, 4 May 2026](https://updates.kickstarter.com/video-games-are-leveling-up-on-kickstarter/)
- Official stats page, archived 1 Jan 2026. The **Games category includes tabletop**, so it is not video games alone.
  - 52.29% success rate.
  - Of 53,572 funded Games projects, 6,684 raised under $1k (12.5%), 23,489 raised $1k-9,999 (43.8%), and 8,143 raised $10k-19,999 (15.2%). The percentages are my calculation.
  - Source: [Kickstarter Stats via Wayback, 1 Jan 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260101190407/https://www.kickstarter.com/help/stats)
  - Conflict: Kicksonar reports a 74.2% success rate and a $4K median for funded Games campaigns, with tabletop included. It uses its own dataset, which differs from Kickstarter's official 52%. — [Kicksonar](https://kicksonar.com/categories/games)

#### Publishers: the advance data
- **Voyer Law 2026 report** (130+ agreements, 2017-2026), via Legal Moves:
  - Average advance **$602,818**, median **$270,000**. Smallest advance **$20,000**, largest over $6M.
  - Average developer revenue share on advance deals: 57.9%.
  - Full-stop recoupment fell from 48.4% to 37%.
  - Audit rights on no-advance deals rose from 47.1% to 66.7%.
  - Source: [Legal Moves Law Firm](https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/state-game-publishing-agreements)
- **Voyer Law 2025 report** (100+ deals):
  - Average advance $674,861, median $300,000, minimum $20,000. 95.5% paid upfront.
  - Median developer share is **50%** with an advance, **70%** without one, and 60% for console porting deals.
  - Developers kept the IP in 96.4% of advance deals.
  - Sources: [GameDev Reports, 12 May 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/voyer-law-publishing-agreements-in); [games.gg](https://games.gg/news/publishing-agreements-in-2025-voyer-law-analysis/)
- Voyer's sample covers deals that reached a games lawyer, so it is skewed towards larger deals. Small no-advance or services deals often never reach one. This is a GitHub research note, not a primary source. — [GitHub research note](https://github.com/KazooKat/hiking-game-research/blob/main/docs/09-gap-funding-publishing.md)
- **Raw Fury** published its terms: 100% recoup, 50/50 split after recoup, and a 15% markup on the advance. GameDiscoverCo's Simon Carless called the markup "aggressive". — [SteamPageAnalyzer, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-to-pitch-game-publishers)
  - Raw Fury's /submissions/ and /pitch/ pages returned 404 on 1 Aug 2026, according to an unverified secondary note. — [GitHub research note](https://github.com/KazooKat/hiking-game-research/blob/main/docs/09-gap-funding-publishing.md)
- Rebekah Saltsman (Finji, 2025): a US$250,000 ask is "a drop in the bucket", yet companies consider it not "profitable enough to fund". She calls this a "survival era" for indies. — [SteamPageAnalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-publisher-vs-self-publishing)
- SUPERJUMP, Oct 2026, quoting developer Johannes Grünewald: "Most smaller publishers don't fund the production of games anymore". The article adds: "if you're releasing your first game, it's very hard to get any real money from publishers." — [SUPERJUMP, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/state-of-indie-2026-part-3-finance-and-tech)
- **Akupara Games** lists "Production financing & flexible milestone-based development funding", with no amounts. It looks for "standout and impactful narratives". — [Akupara Publishing](https://www.akuparagames.com/publishing)
- **Fellow Traveller** is a "story-rich" narrative label (LudoNarraCon), which is a weak fit for a co-op puzzle platformer. It publishes no terms. — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/fellow-traveller)
- **Armor Games Studios** publishes on PC, console, mobile and VR and has a developer intake. I found no amounts. — [Armor Games Studios](https://armorgamesstudios.com/)

#### Indie funds and investors
- **Outersloth** (Innersloth):
  - Will "most likely fund things between **$50k-$2m**, but feel free to shoot your shot". The model is recoup plus revenue share.
  - Funds premium PC, console or mobile games that are **not yet released**; "Early Access games will not be considered". No blockchain, web3 or AI-generated games.
  - The pitch deck must include a budget and production schedule.
  - Team size and country are not specified.
  - Source: [Innersloth: Outersloth](https://www.innersloth.com/outersloth)
  - Published contract: 50% of net revenue to the fund until recouped, then 15%. Over $19M invested across about 24 projects. — [gamedev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/2054-outersloth-reveals-its-game-funding-agreement-with-developers/); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/investment/item-45863)
- **WINGS** (Stockholm):
  - Invests **up to US$500,000** in premium PC and console indie games.
  - **Teams must be founded or co-founded by women or gender-marginalised developers.**
  - Standard terms: 50/50 until recouped once, then 15% to WINGS. Developers keep their IP and publishing rights.
  - Apply with a pitch deck, budget breakdown, demo, marketing plan and sales expectations. No application rounds; first reply within 1-2 weeks.
  - Sources: [WINGS FAQ](https://www.wingsfund.me/faq); [WINGS: Work with WINGS](https://www.wingsfund.me/work-with-wings)
  - **Finishing funds**: for teams with a budget of "**$100,000 or less** and the ability to launch within three to six months" (CEO Astrid Refstrup). WINGS "makes investments for profit". — [Game Developer, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/wings-really-really-wants-to-fund-your-neat-indie-video-game)
  - The WINGS award at IGDX (Indonesia, Oct 2026) pays a **€2,000** prize, about US$2,300 at an assumed 1.15 USD/EUR. — [WINGS LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/company/wings-interactive)
- **Indie Fund**: active. It has funded 55 games with 17 investors and backed new games in Feb 2026 ("Whisk!") and 23 Aug 2026 ("Slime in the Ice Machine"). — [Indie Fund](https://indie-fund.com/)
- **Kowloon Nights**: "a global fund... project-based funding to talented teams for PC/console". It partners with Kepler Interactive. — [LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/company/kowloonnights); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon_Nights)
  - One aggregator lists an "average round investment: 15m USD". That figure is unverified. — [Nordic9](https://nordic9.com/companies/kowloon-nights-investor8859997971)
- **Global Game Jam "Small Projects, Big Impact"** (with Endless Foundation, announced May 2026): pilot micro-grants of **up to US$2,500** for each of at least 25 indie studios over a year.
  - The money pays for short paid micro-projects (under 48 h: assets, QA) done by GGJ community members aged 18+. GGJ handles payments, so this is not cash for founders.
  - Source: [Game Developer, 28 May 2026](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/global-game-jam-launches-micro-grant-program-to-support-indies-and-emerging-talent)
  - The GGJ programme page URL I tried returned 404.

#### Platform programmes and storefronts: no cash, but they set costs
- **ID@Xbox**: registered developers get two dev kits at no cost plus documentation, and no fees to update games. This is not funding. — [Wikipedia: ID@Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ID@Xbox)
  - In Jan 2026 a publisher reported ID@Xbox dev-kit delays after approval. — [Windows Central, 3 Jan 2026](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/indies-idxbox/indie-studio-says-xbox-is-encouraging-development-for-its-platform-yet-id-xbox-dev-kit-delays-are-leaving-approved-projects-stuck-in-limbo)
- **Steam Direct**: US$100 per app. It is non-refundable but recouped once the product reaches $1,000 Adjusted Gross Revenue. — [Steamworks: Steam Direct Fee](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee)
- **itch.io**: open revenue sharing. The seller sets itch.io's cut anywhere from 0-100%; the default is **10%**. — [itch.io, 2015](https://itch.io/updates/introducing-open-revenue-sharing); [itch.io payments docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments)
- **Patreon**: creator pages published after 4 Aug 2025 pay a **10%** platform fee plus processing. Card processing is 2.9% + $0.30 (USD); non-US PayPal is 3.9% + $0.30. — [Patreon Help: Creator fees overview](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/11111747095181-Creator-fees-overview); [Patreon Help: standard platform fee](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/36426991446797-A-standard-platform-fee-for-new-creators-effective-after-August-4-2025)

### Inferences
- **Epic MegaGrants**: rule it out for a Godot 4 game unless the team commits to porting to Unreal, which makes no sense for a 12-week polish. A reviewer will likely flag it if it appears in the budget. I am inferring this from the page's wording, since the FAQ answer did not render.
- **Kickstarter** is the only engine- and company-agnostic money source that suits a US$5k-15k ask.
  - Fee drag on a $10k raise with about 300 pledges: about $500 (5%) + $300 (3%) + $90 ($0.30 × 300), or about **$890 (about 9%)**, before reward fulfilment. My arithmetic, using US rates.
  - With about a 32% video-game success rate, the budget should treat it as upside, not base funding.
  - It works only if a founder can verify as a resident of one of the 25 supported countries.
  - A 12-week schedule leaves little room for the usual pre-launch audience building plus a 30-day campaign. Inference; I found no source on campaign lead times.
- **Publishers**: the smallest advance in Voyer's 130+-deal sample (US$20k) is above a US$5-15k ask, and 2026 commentary says first-time teams struggle to get production money. A no-advance marketing or services deal (median 70% to the developer) is more realistic than an advance for a finished game. Akupara and Armor Games Studios are better genre fits than Fellow Traveller.
- **Outersloth**:
  - US$5-15k is far below its stated US$50k floor ("shoot your shot" leaves the door ajar).
  - The game is already playable on itch.io. Outersloth does not fund released or Early Access games, so the team should describe the itch build as a free prototype or demo. Whether Outersloth counts it as released is not stated.
- **WINGS finishing funds** are the best timing fit (budget ≤ US$100k, launch in 3-6 months), but only if a founder is a woman or gender-marginalised developer. Otherwise the team is not eligible.
- **Prizes**: IGF and BIC are lottery-like upside on top of the budget. Their timing also misses a 12-week window: IGF awards in March 2027, and BIC 2026 is over, with the 2027 call expected around April if the 2026 pattern repeats (inference). The IGF $1,000 finalist travel stipend covers travel, not development.
- **Steam Next Fest / ID@Xbox**: list them as $0 cash. Their value is visibility and console access.

### Gaps
- No official, current minimum deal size was found for Devolver, Whitethorn, Super Rare Games, Raw Fury, Akupara or Armor Games. Super Rare is known for physical editions, but I did not verify its terms this pass.
- Indie Fund's current deal size and terms were not retrieved. Kowloon Nights' minimum ticket is unknown; only an unverified "average $15m" exists.
- Not researched or not found:
  - IGF 2027 submission deadline and entry fee.
  - Indie Prize (Casual Connect) and Day of the Devs (whether there is cash or a fee).

### Inferences
- **Credible total ask** for the writer: 2 founders × US$2,000 × 2.76 months ≈ **US$11,000**, plus the external art, audio and release costs from the existing release_costs.md / people_costs.md, plus 10-30% contingency.
  - If founders work half-time, the stipend line falls to about $5,500.
  - Either way it sits in the US$5k-15k band, which matches Ismail's sense that small first projects run far below publisher-scale budgets.
- **How to frame funding**:
  - Primary: founders' own funds.
  - Secondary: a Kickstarter or pre-order campaign only if an eligible founder runs it and audience-building starts early.
  - Opportunistic: IGF / BIC / A MAZE submissions; WINGS if eligible; a no-advance publisher marketing deal.
  - Explicitly **not**: Epic MegaGrants (Unreal-only), or Outersloth and Kowloon Nights (deal sizes far above the ask).
